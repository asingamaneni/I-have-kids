# Worksheet design principles

Learning Worktable uses an original, child-centered workbook system. Pages should make one learning intention obvious while leaving enough space for a young child to manipulate, point, speak, draw, and write.

Reusable principles:

- name one clear learning focus;
- give short, read-aloud-friendly directions;
- begin with a concrete model or worked example;
- move from guided participation to independent practice;
- mix selected, spoken, drawn, manipulated, and written responses where appropriate;
- use generous answer lines, boxes, sorting areas, and sequence controls;
- provide a short check for comparable evidence only after exploration and practice;
- keep adult answer and evaluation guidance outside the child page;
- use high contrast and distinctions that remain clear in grayscale;
- keep every passage, prompt, exercise, illustration, and composition original.

The sequence is flexible rather than grade-locked. A child may explore a concept early, revisit it through concrete materials, or move ahead when evidence shows readiness. Worksheets reinforce and document learning; they do not define the child's ceiling.
